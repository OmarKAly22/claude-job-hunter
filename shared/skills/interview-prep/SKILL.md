# Interview preparation

**Sources:** `career-profile.md`, the job posting, the user's own stories (collected in step 3), and public company information. Nothing else.

## 1. Collect the facts of the interview
From the invitation email, calendar entry or the user: company, role, round (recruiter screen, hiring manager, technical, case, panel, final), date, time and time zone, length, format, interviewers (name and role). If a link to the posting exists, read it in full.

## 2. Company brief (one screen)
Research with web search and the company's own site, and cite sources:
- What they sell, to whom, and how they make money, in two sentences.
- Size, headquarters, offices relevant to the role, funding or ownership.
- Recent news (last 6 months): launches, funding, acquisitions, layoffs, leadership changes.
- The team behind the role and the problem it solves (from the posting).
- Each interviewer: current role and focus (public profile only; nothing private).
Present facts as they are; never guess numbers.

## 3. Story bank (STAR)
Build 6–10 stories from the user's real experience, each mapped to the posting's requirements:

| Story | Requirements it proves | Situation | Task | Action (what *the user* did) | Result (confirmed numbers only) | 30-second version |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover at least: a hard technical decision, a production incident or failure, disagreement with a stakeholder, leading without authority, a delivery under pressure, something learned fast, and the one project that best fits this role.

**Honesty rules (non-negotiable):**
- The user's actions are described as they happened. A design decision that existed from the start is never re-told as something the user "proposed" or "convinced others of", unless the user says so.
- Results only from the profile's confirmed numbers or numbers the user states now.
- Anything the user did not do is phrased as "what I would do" or "the next step would be", never as done.
- Known gaps are answered honestly: what they did instead, what transfers, how they would close the gap.

## 4. Likely questions with speakable answers
For the round type, list the 10–15 most likely questions. For each give a short answer the user can say out loud in under 90 seconds, in plain spoken language, plus the story to use. Always include:
- "Tell me about yourself" (60–90 s, ending on why this role).
- "Why this company?" and "Why are you leaving?" (positive, no criticism of the current employer).
- The gap questions this posting will raise.
- Salary: by default defer politely to later in the process and ask for the range, unless the profile says otherwise.
- Notice period and start date from the profile.
Write the answers as clean study text: no hedges, notes or placeholders in what the user is meant to say.

## 5. Technical deep dive (technical rounds)
For each core technology or system in the posting that the user has worked with: a simple explanation, how the user used it, trade-offs and alternatives, typical follow-up questions with short answers, and one diagram if it helps. For posting topics the user has not worked with: a short, correct primer and an honest bridge ("I have not used X in production; I used Y, which solves the same problem by...").

## 6. Questions to ask (5–8)
Specific to the team and round: success in the first 90 days, the biggest current problem, how decisions are made, team setup, what the next round looks like. Never questions answered on the company's website.

## 7. Deliver
- One document (or doc artifact) with: interview facts, company brief, story bank, questions and answers, deep dives, questions to ask, and a 10-line cheat sheet for the last 5 minutes before the call.
- If the user wants both languages (e.g. English and German), every section exists in both, with the same content.
- Privacy: never mention the user's other interview processes, salary history or confidential project details in any answer meant for the interviewer.

## 8. After the interview
Offer a short debrief: what went well, questions that were hard, what to add to the story bank, and a thank-you note in the user's voice (drafted only, never sent).
